Full-duplex models listen and speak at the same time, yet they are still evaluated one at a time, against a partner that cannot react. DyaFDB lets two of them talk to each other with cooperative or conflicting goals, and scores both.

Abstract

Full-duplex spoken dialogue models listen and speak at the same time, enabling voice agents to have natural, low-latency interactions that turn-based systems cannot offer. However, they are commonly evaluated against single-sided interlocutors: pre-recorded audio that cannot react, or an automated examiner that reacts in real time but only administers a fixed sequence of tests and is never graded. These single-sided frameworks evaluate only half of a two-body problem, where turn-taking, overlap, and interruption are joint products of two coupled speakers. We propose DyaFDB, a framework that evaluates full-duplex models in a dyadic setup: two models converse directly under assigned roles with cooperative or conflicting goals, and both sides are scored offline with an external judge. DyaFDB probes how the two models behave toward each other, such as how they take turns or carry an assigned role under different interests. We instantiate four tasks as 140 scenarios and record 7,560 conversations, covering six self- and cross-play pairings. Throughout the experiments, we observe that how a model behaves continually reshapes its partner. We thus demonstrate that each model must be both the examiner and examinee of the other, and no single fixed interlocutor can play both parts. We will release the scenarios, role prompts, and recording protocols between two full-duplex models, without any pre-recorded audio.

Turn-taking is a joint product of two speakers

Turn-taking, overlap, and interruption are joint products of two coupled speakers. A framework that fixes one side evaluates only half of the two-body problem.

Pre-recorded audio

The model reacts to a recording that cannot react back. Same input whatever the model says. One side graded.

Automated examiner

A real-time speech model asks follow-ups and can interrupt, but it administers a fixed sequence of tests and is never graded.

DyaFDB

Two full-duplex models converse on equal standing, each with a private role in a shared situation. Both are scored offline from the recording.

A bridge on a lockstep clock

Each model's output stream becomes the other's input stream. No examiner, no privileged channel.

80 msone frame per tick; at tick t each model hears what its partner emitted at t−1
60 sper conversation; the opener gets a 1.5 s lead-in while the partner only listens
6 pairingsthree open models, each with itself and with the others; roles and speaking order swapped
offline scoringmechanical measures on the stereo recording plus an LLM judge on an anonymized transcript

What the dyadic setup reveals

Who decides a turn-taking metric

Cutting into a pause is the model's own disposition (η²model = 0.74). Taking the turn at the end depends on how the partner hands it over (η²partner = 0.65).

Limited cooperators

Beside a digressing partner PersonaPlex loses its role (0.51 → 0.29) while Raon holds it more firmly (0.68 → 0.83). In information pooling only teams with Raon beat random choice.

Conflict scores depend on who it faces

Raon, best in every Coordination task, is the weakest in both holding roles. The same defender leaks 39% of its secrets against one attacker and 63% against another.
